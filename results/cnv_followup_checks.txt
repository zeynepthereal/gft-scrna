OVERALL SUMMARY (facts only, no interpretation beyond the numbers above)
##########################################################################################

CHECK A (Patient2):
- 3 of 4 clones (0, 1, 2 = 68% of carcinoma-sample epithelial cells) are normal-like: CNV
  score not clearly above the normal reference or too few significant arm events, and normal
  colon markers (CA1/CA2/SLC26A3/AQP8/GUCA2A) closer to the normal reference row than to
  clone 3. Only clone 3 (1056 cells, 32%) is malignant-like (CNV score 2.9x normal median,
  7/7 arm events significant).
- This partly explains the earlier "neither hypothesis cleanly satisfied" verdict: a
  substantial share of what was clustered as "carcinoma epithelial" is CNV-wise
  indistinguishable from normal epithelium (likely adjacent normal tissue captured in the
  carcinoma sample, or doublets/ambient contamination -- this analysis cannot distinguish
  those causes).
- Restricted to the single malignant clone, residual is still highly heterogeneous
  (std=3.53, range 2.36-27.16) -- CNV clone membership does not collapse residual to a tight
  distribution even within one genetic clone.
- ASCL2 is NOT positively associated with residual inside the malignant clone: Spearman
  rho=-0.129 (p=2.7e-5), weak and negative. %ASCL2+ is actually higher in the bottom-decile
  (67.0%) than the top-decile (53.8%) residual cells within this clone. This complicates the
  original Patient2 narrative that linked high residual to ASCL2+ status -- within the
  confirmed-malignant population, that link does not hold; ASCL2 is broadly expressed
  (60-70%+) across the residual spectrum in this clone rather than concentrated in the
  highest-residual cells.

CHECK B (Patient1):
- Correlation-distance/average-linkage merging (threshold corr>0.9) collapses the 7 clones
  to 3 merged clones: merged1 (n=510, weak 8q/20q gain only), merged2 (n=2252, full
  8q+/20q+/8p-/17p- profile, 68% of cells), merged3 (n=210, only weak 8p loss).
- eta^2 DECREASES after merging (0.0146 vs 0.0708 for the original 7 clones) -- merged CNV
  clone identity explains even less of residual variance than the finer clustering did. This
  rules out over-splitting as the reason the original analysis found a low eta^2; if
  anything, the finer 7-clone split captured slightly more clone-related residual structure
  than the coarser 3-clone merge.
- Max top-decile concentration rises to 84.9% (merged2), but this is a size artifact: merged2
  alone holds 68% of all cells, so a proportional share of top-decile cells there is expected
  (expected 225.8, observed 253, obs/exp=1.12 -- barely above proportional, not a genuine
  concentration). The other two merged clones are also near their proportional (obs/exp
  0.51-1.4 range not shown at merged level but consistent with the small deviations here).
- Net effect: merging does not change the Patient1 verdict in the clone direction; if
  anything it reinforces that finer CNV substructure (7 clones) was not manufacturing the
  earlier low eta^2 -- the true merged-clone-level eta^2 is even lower.

CHECK C (both patients):
- Residual correlates NEGATIVELY, not positively, with S_score and G2M_score in both
  patients (Patient1: rho=-0.325/-0.403; Patient2: rho=-0.140/-0.105, all p<1e-8).
- Top-decile residual cells are LESS often in a cycling phase (S or G2M) than the rest:
  Patient1 7.7% cycling in top-decile vs 32.8% in the rest; Patient2 15.4% vs 21.4%.
- This rules out cell-cycle/proliferation as a confound inflating the high-residual, ASCL2+
  population identified earlier -- the opposite pattern is observed. High-residual carcinoma
  cells skew toward G1 (non-cycling) rather than S/G2M, at least by these standard cell-cycle
  gene-score markers.
