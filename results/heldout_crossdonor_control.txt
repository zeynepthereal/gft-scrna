in MOST samples, malignant/held-out-normal ratios are CLEARLY above 1 (>1.2x) AND
clearly exceed the typical (median) cross-donor normal ratio for that dataset. If a
malignant cell deviates no more than a healthy cell from a different person, the claim
fails for that sample.

================================================================================================
CAVEATS
================================================================================================
- GSE161277 has only 2 donors -- its cross-donor distribution (2 ordered pairs) is not
  statistically meaningful and is reported for completeness only, not as a robust
  baseline the way the 7-donor CRC cohort distribution is.
- Malignant cells' held-out residual is an AVERAGE over 5 different fold-fits per cell
  (since malignant cells are never in the training set regardless of fold); normal
  cells get exactly one held-out value each, from the single fold where they were
  excluded. This asymmetry is a reasonable way to put both groups on equal out-of-
  sample footing, but is not identical machinery for both groups.
- CNV clone labels (GSE161277 malignant-like cells) and the residual are derived from
  the same expression matrix -- internal consistency, not independent ground truth.
- Cross-donor comparisons mix different individuals, sequencing runs and possibly batch
  effects; a large cross-donor ratio does not distinguish biological inter-individual
  variation from technical batch variation.

ADDITIONAL OBSERVATION (facts only, not part of the pre-stated decision rule, but relevant)
---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The decision rule above compares each sample's malignant ratio to the cross-donor MEDIAN. A
stricter comparison -- against the cross-donor MAX (the largest healthy-person-to-healthy-person
deviation observed) -- gives a different picture for the two datasets:
- GSE161277 (Patient1, Patient2): malignant ratios (3.77x, 2.09x) exceed even the cross-donor MAX
  (1.50x) in both samples. Clean separation from person-to-person variation.
- CRC_epithelial: malignant ratios exceed the cross-donor MEDIAN (2.32x) in 6/7 donors, but do NOT
  exceed the cross-donor MAX (5.58x) in ANY of the 7 donors. The largest healthy-vs-healthy
  cross-donor deviation observed in this cohort (HTA8_6006 scored on HTA8_6018's subspace, 5.58x)
  is larger than every donor's malignant/held-out-normal ratio except HTA8_6018 itself (5.30x,
  still below 5.58x). Read plainly: in the CRC cohort, the single largest residual deviation
  measured anywhere in Test 1/Test 2 came from one healthy donor's normal cells scored on another
  healthy donor's subspace, not from a malignant cell.
This does not overturn the SURVIVES verdict above (which used the median, as the decision rule
specified), but it means the CRC cohort's separation between "malignant" and "different healthy
person" is not clean at the extremes -- cross-donor (batch/individual) variation in this cohort
can rival or exceed malignant deviation in magnitude, even though malignant cells are higher than
their own held-out normal reference in most donors.
