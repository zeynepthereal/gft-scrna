##########################################################################################
CAVEATS AND ADDITIONAL OBSERVATIONS (facts only, no interpretation beyond the numbers above)
##########################################################################################

TEST A:
- By the stated decision rule, 8/9 samples show the malignant-vs-normal residual difference
  surviving n_genes stratification -- the core claim SURVIVES in most samples. The one
  exception, HTA8_6006, shows essentially NO effect once matched on n_genes: median n_genes
  is identical between its normal and malignant groups (766 vs 766) and every quintile ratio
  is exactly 1.00x (OLS is_malignant coefficient = -0.0000, p=1.000).
- n_genes_by_counts and malignant status are strongly collinear in most samples, which limits
  how cleanly "controlled" this test really is. In Patient1, normal median n_genes=1603 vs
  malignant=4400 (2.7x); in several CRC donors the gap is similarly large (e.g. HTA8_6013:
  2669 vs 4988; HTA8_6018: 2853 vs 4788). Because of this, the highest n_genes quintile was
  skipped (<20 cells/group) in 5/9 samples (Patient1, HTA8_6002, HTA8_6013, HTA8_6018,
  HTA8_6028) -- the two groups barely overlap at the high end of the n_genes distribution, so
  the "stratified" comparison there relies on fewer, more extreme quintiles, and the OLS
  regression extrapolates through a region with limited normal/malignant overlap. The p-values
  and coefficients are large and consistent enough that this is unlikely to be pure confound,
  but the collinearity itself means n_genes cannot be fully separated from malignant status in
  this data, only partially adjusted for.
- The direction of the log(n_genes) coefficient is not consistent across samples: negative in
  Patient1, Patient2, and HTA8_6002 (more genes -> lower residual, after accounting for
  malignant status), but positive in HTA8_6013/6016/6018/6028 (more genes -> higher residual).
  This inconsistency was not evident before stratifying and is reported as-is.

TEST B:
- Residual's AUROC for malignant-like vs normal-like (0.769 Patient1, 0.889 Patient2) is
  higher than the two designed baselines (Euclidean distance to normal centroid: 0.737, 0.597;
  inverted normal-colon-marker mean: 0.463, 0.638) in both patients, most clearly so in
  Patient2.
- However, n_genes_by_counts ALONE reaches AUROC=0.738 in Patient1 -- essentially identical to
  residual's 0.769, and higher than the Euclidean-centroid baseline. In Patient1, a substantial
  share of residual's ability to separate CNV-confirmed malignant-like from normal-like cells
  is matched by gene-count alone; residual does not clearly outperform this simple confound
  there. In Patient2, residual (0.889) does more clearly exceed n_genes alone (0.786), a wider
  margin than in Patient1.
- The normal-colon-marker score is a weak classifier and, in Patient1, is worse than random
  (AUROC=0.463 < 0.5) even after inverting it -- it does not usefully separate malignant-like
  from normal-like cells in Patient1 by this metric, despite being designed to.
- As stated in the note before the numbers: CNV clone labels and projection residual are both
  derived from the same expression matrix through overlapping preprocessing (same cells, same
  PCA), so none of these AUROC values should be read as validating residual against an
  independent ground truth -- they describe internal consistency between two measures computed
  from the same data, not predictive validity on new data.
