SUMMARY (facts only, no interpretation beyond the numbers above)
- CNV signal is present and CRC-typical: 7/7 expected arm events detected, all p<1e-20.
  Global CNV score is actually slightly LOWER in carcinoma than normal (ratio 0.85x,
  p=6.3e-6) even though arm-level tests are individually significant -- the global score
  averages over the whole genome including many unaffected regions, while the arm tests
  isolate the affected arms; both are reported as computed, without adjustment.
- Only 4 CNV clones at the lowest passing resolution (0.3). Just one clone (clone 3, n=1056)
  carries the CRC-typical arm profile (7+/8q+/13+/20q+/8p-); the other 3 clones (0, 1, 2)
  show no notable arm-level event by the |mean|>0.01 threshold.
- Top-decile residual cells ARE concentrated: 73.1% fall in clone 3, which is also the only
  clone with the CNV-typical profile and by far the highest ASCL2+ rate (68.9% vs 0.9-16.2%
  in the other clones) and highest median residual (5.52 vs 2.52-3.22).
- CNV clone explains a moderate-to-large share of residual variance: eta^2=0.252 (ANOVA),
  i.e. ~25% of residual variance is between-clone.
- Spearman(residual, cnv_score)=0.621, p~0: stronger correlation than Patient1.
- By the stated decision rules: max clone concentration (73.1%) exceeds the 70% clone
  threshold, but eta^2 (0.252) does not clear the >0.5 bar the clone rule also requires;
  it also does not fall under the <0.3 bar the state rule requires for "small" between-clone
  variance -- 0.252 sits just under that boundary while direction (concentration) points to
  clone. Verdict printed above: neither rule set is cleanly satisfied; the crosstab and
  ASCL2 pattern (d, e) lean toward a single dominant CNV clone carrying most high-residual,
  ASCL2+ cells, more clone-like than Patient1's pattern, but eta^2 alone does not meet the
  clone threshold.

CAVEAT on arm-level test (b): "present" means direction-vs-normal matches the expected event
AND p<0.01 -- it does not require the carcinoma value itself to be clearly positive (gain) or
negative (loss). Chr17p in Patient2 is flagged "present" (loss) but its carcinoma value is
close to zero (-0.0002) and only marginally more negative than normal (0.0005); a weak signal
despite the significant p-value, likely driven by large cell numbers rather than a strong
effect.
